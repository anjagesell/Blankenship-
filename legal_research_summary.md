## Key Legal Principles

### 1. Truth as an Absolute Defense
- **Truth is a complete defense to defamation claims** in the United States
- If the published information is true, it generally cannot be actionable even if it harms someone's reputation
- The burden of proof lies on the plaintiff (person suing) to show that statements are FALSE

### 2. First Amendment Protection
- **Strong protection for truthful statements on matters of public concern**
- Criminal justice issues and wrongful convictions are matters of public interest
- Publishing truthful court documents and evidence is protected speech
- The public has a First Amendment right of access to criminal trials and associated documents

### 3. Defamation/Libel Law Requirements
For someone to successfully sue you for defamation, they must prove:
- The statement is **FALSE** (truth defeats this immediately)
- The statement was published to third parties
- The statement caused harm to reputation
- You were at fault (negligence or actual malice)

**Public Interest Standard:** For matters of public concern (like wrongful conviction cases), plaintiffs must prove "actual malice" - meaning you knew the information was false OR had reckless disregard for the truth.

### 4. Privacy Law Considerations
- Using real names without consent CAN implicate privacy rights
- However, privacy claims fail when:
  - Information is lawfully obtained (public court records)
  - Information is truthful
  - Information addresses matters of public interest
  
**Your Situation:** Court records, evidence in a criminal case, and documentation of wrongful conviction are matters of public interest and are typically public record.

### 5. Password-Protected Website Factor
- Password protection provides some additional legal protection
- It demonstrates you're not broadly publishing to harm reputation
- It shows the purpose is documentation rather than malicious defamation
- Limited access reduces the "publication" element of defamation

## Specific Risk Assessment

### ✅ LOW RISK Scenarios:
1. **Publishing court documents with real names** - Public records, truthful, public interest
2. **Publishing factual evidence** - Documents, photos, records that prove the case
3. **Stating verifiable facts** - Dates, events that occurred, quotes from official documents
4. **Documenting timeline of events** - What happened when, supported by evidence

### ⚠️ MODERATE RISK Scenarios:
1. **Making interpretations or conclusions** - "This proves X was lying" vs. "Document Y contradicts statement Z"
2. **Including private individuals not directly involved** - Limit to key parties in the case
3. **Emotional or inflammatory language** - Stick to factual, documentary style

### 🛑 HIGH RISK Scenarios (AVOID):
1. **Making statements you cannot prove are true**
2. **Making accusations not supported by evidence**
3. **Publishing private information obtained illegally**
4. **Making threats or statements that could be seen as threats**

❌ **Use inflammatory or emotional language about individuals**  
❌ **Include irrelevant private information**  
❌ **Make threats or intimidating statements**  
❌ **Publish information you obtained illegally**
